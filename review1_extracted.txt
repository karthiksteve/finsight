Abstract
Financial documents such as news articles, quarterly reports, regulatory filings, and press releases contain a large volume of valuable information expressed in unstructured natural language. Analysts, investors, and automated trading or risk-assessment systems increasingly depend on this information, yet manually reading and extracting relevant details from thousands of documents is slow, expensive, and error-prone. This project, FinSight AI, presents a Named Entity Recognition (NER)-centred, transformer-based pipeline for the financial domain, extended into a complete document-analysis platform that also performs sentence-level sentiment analysis (FinBERT), legal/financial clause and risk extraction, and Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) based question answering over uploaded documents. A structured literature survey of fifteen papers (2023-2026) drawn from IEEE, Springer, Elsevier and other reputed venues was carried out - five each covering financial NER, FinBERT-based sentiment analysis, and RAG/clause-extraction - to identify existing gaps such as poor generalization of generic NER models to financial vocabulary, accuracy-efficiency trade-offs in sentiment models, and hallucination/retrieval-quality issues in RAG pipelines over long filings. Based on these gaps, an integrated end-to-end architecture is proposed and partially implemented, combining a custom spaCy/BERT-based NER model, a FinBERT sentiment service, a clause/risk-extraction service, and a RAG-based chatbot behind a single FastAPI backend and React frontend, with results exportable as JSON, CSV or an HTML report.
1. Problem Statement and Objectives
1.1 Problem Statement
Extracting structured financial information from unstructured text is a challenging task because financial language is dense, domain-specific, and often ambiguous. Generic NER and sentiment models trained on general text corpora frequently misclassify or completely miss financial entities such as company names embedded in complex sentences, monetary values in varied formats, and dates written in inconsistent styles; they also struggle to reliably flag risk-bearing clauses or answer natural-language questions about a specific document without hallucinating facts. There is a need for a domain-focused system that reliably converts raw financial and legal-financial text into structured, machine-readable, and query-able insight.
1.2 Objectives
To design, train and evaluate a Named Entity Recognition (NER) model - combined with FinBERT-based sentiment analysis and clause/risk extraction - capable of accurately identifying key financial entities, sentiment, and risk-bearing language from unstructured financial and legal-financial text.
To develop an end-to-end extraction and retrieval pipeline (including a Retrieval-Augmented Generation question-answering module) that transforms raw financial documents into structured, actionable insights, and to evaluate performance using standard metrics such as precision, recall, and F1-score.
2. Introduction
Analysts, investors, and automated trading or risk-assessment systems increasingly depend on financial text, yet manually reading thousands of documents is slow and error-prone. Named Entity Recognition (NER), a core NLP task, addresses this by automatically identifying and classifying key pieces of information - company names, monetary amounts, dates, and stock identifiers - embedded in free-form text. This project focuses on an NER system tailored to the financial domain, which has its own vocabulary, abbreviations, and structural conventions (ticker symbols, currency notations, formal filing language) that cause general-purpose models to under-perform.
3. Motivation
The volume of financial text data (news, filings, reports) is growing rapidly, making manual extraction impractical at scale.
Accurate entity extraction is a foundational step for downstream tasks such as sentiment analysis, risk assessment, fraud detection, and automated report generation.
Domain-specific NER and sentiment models consistently outperform general-purpose models on specialized text, as confirmed by the literature reviewed in Section 6.
There is growing industry demand for automated financial-intelligence pipelines - combining entity extraction, sentiment analysis, clause/risk detection, and document question-answering - that reduce manual analyst workload.
4. Scope of the Project
The scope of this project covers the collection and preprocessing of financial and legal-financial text data, the design and training of one or more NER models, sentence-level sentiment analysis using FinBERT, extraction of risk-bearing clauses, a Retrieval-Augmented Generation question-answering module, and the evaluation of all models against standard NLP performance metrics. The project focuses on core financial entity types - organizations, persons, monetary values, dates, and stock/ticker symbols - and produces a working pipeline, implemented as the FinSight AI platform, that can be extended to additional entity types, clause categories, or larger datasets in future iterations. Full production-grade deployment at scale and real-time streaming ingestion are considered outside the current scope and are noted as directions for future work.

5.3 Dataset
The NER model will be trained and evaluated on a financial text corpus assembled from publicly available sources such as financial news headlines, SEC filings, and annotated financial NER benchmark datasets (e.g., FiNER-ORD, FiNER-139). Text will be labeled with standard entity tags (ORG, PERSON, DATE, MONEY, LOCATION) using the BIO (Begin-Inside-Outside) tagging scheme. Sentiment labels follow the standard positive/negative/neutral scheme used in financial-sentiment benchmarks (e.g., Financial PhraseBank), and clause-extraction data is drawn from CUAD-style contract annotations restricted to finance-relevant clause categories (payment terms, interest rate, termination, liability, governing law).

6. Methodology
The project proceeds in stages, beginning with data collection and annotation, followed by preprocessing and exploratory analysis of the financial text corpus. A baseline NER model is first trained using a traditional sequence-labeling approach (BiLSTM-CRF) to establish reference performance. This is followed by fine-tuning a transformer-based model (BERT/FinBERT) pretrained on financial or general text, expected to capture richer contextual representations of financial language. In parallel, a FinBERT-based sentence-level sentiment classifier and a clause/risk extraction module are developed. Both NER approaches are evaluated on a held-out test set and compared to identify the more effective approach for this domain. The best-performing components are then integrated into the end-to-end extraction pipeline described in Section 5, together with a Retrieval-Augmented Generation module that allows a user to ask natural-language questions about an uploaded document.

8. Summary of Literature, Limitations, and Proposed Alternative Methodology
8.1 Summary of Existing Systems and Identified Gaps
Across the fifteen reviewed papers, three recurring gaps emerge. First, in financial NER (Section 7.1, papers 1-5), transformer models (BERT-MRC, BraFiNER, IndoBERT-BiGRU-CRF) consistently outperform CRF/BiLSTM baselines, but every study is confined to a single language, entity set, or document genre (earnings calls, contracts, or informal chat), and numeric/monetary entities remain error-prone, especially under generative reformulations. Second, in FinBERT-based sentiment analysis (papers 6-10), FinBERT reliably beats generic BERT and lexicon-based methods (e.g., Loughran-McDonald) on financial text, yet accuracy plateaus in the low-to-mid 60-75% range on noisy news data, and combining FinBERT with larger LLMs (OPT, GPT-4) or RoBERTa improves nuance capture only at higher computational cost. Third, in RAG-based question answering and clause/risk extraction (papers 11-15), pure vector-similarity retrieval is prone to hallucination and misses relational facts in long, table-heavy financial filings; hybrid knowledge-graph + vector retrieval and QA-based clause extraction with knowledge distillation improve faithfulness and efficiency respectively, but no reviewed system integrates NER, sentiment, clause-risk scoring, and RAG question-answering into a single coherent platform - each paper addresses only one sub-task in isolation.
8.2 Proposed Alternative Methodology and Design
To address the isolation of these sub-tasks, this project proposes - and has begun implementing as FinSight AI - a unified, multi-service architecture (Fig. 2) rather than a single-purpose NER model. Raw documents (PDF, DOCX, TXT, HTML) are parsed once and routed through four specialized, independently evaluable services behind a common FastAPI orchestrator: (i) a custom spaCy/BERT-based NER service tuned to financial entities, informed by the MRC-style span extraction and knowledge-distillation ideas from papers 1, 4 and 15; (ii) a FinBERT sentiment service, following the comparative evidence from papers 6-9 that FinBERT is the most reliable single model for financial tone; (iii) a clause/risk extraction service, adapting the QA-based clause-extraction methodology of papers 13-14 to finance-specific clauses (interest rate, covenant, collateral) rather than only general contract clauses; and (iv) a Retrieval-Augmented Generation service for document question-answering, informed by the hybrid knowledge-graph + vector retrieval approach of paper 12 to reduce hallucination. Results from all four services are merged into one structured record per document, persisted in PostgreSQL/Azure-Blob-compatible storage, and exportable as JSON, CSV, or an HTML report - giving an analyst one unified dashboard instead of four disconnected tools. This design directly targets the generalization, accuracy-efficiency, and hallucination gaps identified above, while acknowledging (per Section 4) that full production hardening and real-time streaming remain future work.
